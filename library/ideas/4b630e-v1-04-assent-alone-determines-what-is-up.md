# Assent alone determines what is up to us

In Stoic psychology, impressions arise from the world and are not fully under our control, but giving or withholding assent to them is. The author explains that the Stoics thought we are responsible for our assents, not for the impressions we receive. Their analogy of a cylinder and a cone pushed down a slope shows how an external push starts motion while each object's own nature determines how it moves. ==Our character therefore depends entirely on what we assent to==, which the author calls a far-reaching idea in the history of philosophy.
