# Passions are false judgments that can be corrected

The Stoics defined passions as excessive impulses that disobey reason, and they argued that each passion involves a false judgment. Distress, for example, assumes that a present illness is a real bad, when it is merely dispreferred. Since the error lies in judgment rather than in feeling itself, the wise person can aim at freedom from passions. The author notes that the Stoics still allowed good feelings directed at genuine goods, such as virtue.
