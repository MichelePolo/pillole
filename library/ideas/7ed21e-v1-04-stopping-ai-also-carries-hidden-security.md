# Stopping AI also carries hidden security costs

The author argues that slowing down AI for safety reasons has a cost that is less visible than the risks it aims to prevent. AI discoveries in medicine and other sciences may reduce human suffering, and a lack of progress may mean that people who could have been saved die, both now and in future generations. The author notes that a lack of checks may lead to catastrophic outcomes, but insists that stopping AI also has a security cost, which is simply harder to see. ==Safety decisions therefore have to weigh the costs of inaction as well as the risks of action.==
