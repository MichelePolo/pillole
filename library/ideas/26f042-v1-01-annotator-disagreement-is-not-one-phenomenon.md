# Annotator disagreement is not one phenomenon

Disagreement between annotators is often treated as meaningful information, a sign of legitimate differences in how people perceive content. The authors argue this view is too simple. When annotators label toxic text differently, the gap may come from several sources: linguistic ambiguity, missing context, epistemic uncertainty, or problems in the annotation process itself. Only some of these reflect genuine perspectival variation. Others could be reduced with better data or clearer tasks. Treating every disagreement as equally valid risks mistaking avoidable noise for real diversity of judgment. According to the authors, the first step is to identify and characterize these sources separately, before deciding which disagreements to preserve.
